GauTrack: who needs what

The live system, not the laptop demonstration. One computer holds the record; four kinds of user reach it, each at their own address. Nothing is installed on anybody's phone or computer.

What each person needs

WhoOpensOn whatWhat they must be given
Field officerstagging, sightings, impounding /app Any Android phone with mobile data. Their own phone is fine. One account each, never shared. The phone must be allowed to use its camera and location when the page asks.
The officeDMC and staff /admin The office computer, any modern browser. One account each, with a second code at sign-in. Shared logins make the record of who did what worthless.
CM and his office /cm Any computer, from anywhere. One read-only account. Totals only: no names, no phone numbers, no photographs.
General public /report Their own phone. Nothing. No account, no app. The link and a printed code on a poster are the whole rollout.
The computer itself — One machine that stays on: rented in an Indian data centre, or an office machine with a fixed public address. A web address, nightly backups, and one named officer formally responsible for it.

What the office must obtain

  1. A web address. A gov.in name from the IT department, or a bought .in name for about ₹1,000 a year while that is processed.
  2. A computer that never sleeps. Rented in an Indian data centre, or one in the office with a fixed public address and a battery backup.
  3. An account for it in the department’s name, on the office email, on an office payment method.
  4. A list of staff needing logins, each marked field officer, office, or read-only.
  5. A named officer formally answerable for the record once it holds real names.
  6. A security inspection by a government-approved auditor, before any real keeper’s details are entered.

Worth knowing before you are asked

  • Nothing gets installed. No app store, no software on any office computer. Four web addresses is the whole system.
  • A secure address is not optional. A phone will not give a web page its camera or its location unless the connection is secure, so without it the field app cannot photograph or locate anything.
  • The public form has no login on purpose. Public reports are held separately and never counted in the headline figures until an officer verifies them.
  • Old phones are fine. The field app keeps working with no signal and sends what it recorded once the phone is back in coverage.
  • One person, one login. Every entry is signed by the officer who made it. That is what makes the numbers defensible if they are challenged.
